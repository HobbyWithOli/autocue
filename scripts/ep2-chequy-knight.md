## 4. Priming — 1:30 to 2:15

*Clip 2.1: airbrush footage, real time for a few seconds, then sped up. Asset 06 Gaahleri lower third.*

Same start as last week: grey primer, though I'm skipping the zenithal due to the amazing coverage of Tone Pro paints, it seems redundant. All painted through the Gaahleri TurboX Mini. Gaahleri sent me this for free, and there's an affiliate link in the description if you want one. Ten Men-at-Arms and a knight in, it's still going strong.

## 5. Metals — 2:15 to 3:00

*Clips 2.2 to 2.4: close-up on the helmet and lance tip. Asset 03 paint swatches on screen from here.*

Metals first. Vallejo Air Steel, then a wash of Black Templar Contrast. It drops into the recesses and makes the armour look properly heavy. A quick edge highlight of Steel or drybrush for the chainmail brings the shine back. This is gonna be easy!

## 6. The shield — 3:00 to 5:30

*Clip 2.5: the money shot. Overhead camera, locked off, so the Short can be cut from the same footage.*

Right. I can't put it off any longer. The squares. Two thin coats of Hawkshroud Yellow over the primer first.

Last week I learned the hard way that pencil on a shield is a mistake, so this time I'm cutting masking tape into thin strips and laying them across the shield to mask off the squares that stay gold. [Reaction to how fiddly the tape was.]

If you want to follow this army all the way to the battlefield, subscribe. A new episode lands every Friday, and apparently you all enjoy watching me suffer.

Then the blue goes on between the strips: Scinari Blue. Two coats, peel the tape, and a lot of counting. [How long it took.]

The top-left four squares are what's known as a canton, often added to a family's arms as a mark of honour or to show a link to another family. I'm painting this in Bloodtoof Red. That's the only big bit of red on the whole model, and it's what makes the chequerboard look like heraldry instead of a picnic blanket.

Then I tidy the edges with a bit more yellow, and give the steel rim the same treatment as the armour.

## 7. Surcoat, caparison and pennant — 5:30 to 7:00

*Clips 2.6, 2.13 and 2.16: the blue quarters on the rider, the caparison airbrushed gold, then the blue quarters brushed on. Clip 2.15 for the pennant.*

The surcoat and the horse's caparison are quartered: blue, gold, gold, blue. Scinari Blue for the blue, with tones two and three to bring up the folds, and Hawkshroud Yellow for the gold, with tone two in the shadows. The caparison is a big area, so the gold went on with the airbrush.

The red stays as an accent: just the pennant. Too much red and he stops looking like he belongs with the Duke.

## 8. The horse — 7:00 to 8:15

*Clips 2.17, 2.18 and 2.21: horse on its own, then held next to the rider.*

For the horse I went grey, and here's why. Most of my horse recipes use Carthalos Skin, which is also my leather colour, so the reins would vanish into a brown horse. A grey horse lets the leather stand out on its own, and the blue and gold quarters on the caparison really pop against it.

Dataslate Grey 4 for the coat and Dataslate Grey 3 for the mane and tail, and the saddle and reins in Carthalos Skin. [Add the Dataslate 2 shade, Blackstone Void highlights or muzzle glaze here if you did them.]

## 9. The eagles — 8:15 to 9:15

*Clip 2.8 for the helmet crest. Clips 2.10, 2.11 and 2.14: macro on the pet eagle. Asset 09 Name the eagle.*

There are actually two eagles on this knight. The one on his helmet is metal: Steel, Black Templar, then a little ochre and orange-ochre to warm it up.

And he has a pet eagle, because a knight with a chequerboard shield clearly needed more to paint.

Gnarlwood 2 for the feathers, Gnarlwood 1 and Shading Medium in the recesses, then Gnarlwood 4 on the edge of every feather and a touch of Khemri Sands on the tips. The back of the head gets that golden-eagle glow: Ta'unar Ochre up to Khemri Sands. Beak and feet in Hawkshroud Yellow, black talons, and a yellow eye with a black dot. [Name for the eagle, or ask the comments for one.]
