## 5. Priming — 3:20 to 4:50

*Men-at-Arms on handles. A few seconds in real time, then speed it up.*

Step one, prime. Ten models with big flat tabards is exactly the job you want a workhorse airbrush like this for.

Im using Vallejo grey primer, unthinned, straight out of the pot. It sprays great, and I Highly recommend it.

And what about the airbrush? It took the paint really well. It did sputter now and again, but nothing that ruined a coat. All ten men at arms primed in about ten minutes, all in.

## 6. Zenithal — 4:50 to 6:10

*Spray from directly above, like the sun.*

Next, zenithal. If you've not heard of it, it just means spraying a lighter colour from above, like sunlight. Think of it like an Ordnance Survey map: the higher up the model, the lighter it gets. That one's for you, Phil.

It shows you where the highlights go before you've even picked up a brush, and that's going to make the next bit much easier.

I'm going white from above, because that's going to make the yellow pop later. Now, white over grey is hard to see on camera, so you'll have to trust me a bit here. But I didn't hit any problems, and it laid down a nice soft coat from above.

*Side by side: primed vs zenithal. Turn the model so the light reads from the top.*

## 7. Tone Pro and the Duke's colours — 6:10 to 8:10

*Tone Pro bottles lined up 1 to 5. Graphic: 1 = darkest, 3 = midtone, 5 = lightest.*

Now a new airbrush would normally be enough to get me hyped for a project, but excitingly I've also got my hands on another tool that's going to aid me in my campaign against the greenskins, the pointy ears and the sex-fiends. Warhammer Tone Pro. This is Games Workshops newest paint range. Every colour comes in a pentat of five tones, numbered one to five. One's the darkest, five's the lightest, three is usually your midtone. So instead of raiding four different ranges for a base, a shade and two highlights, you just go down the numbers. Even I can count to five. Mostly.

If you're following along at home, here are the paints you'll need.

*On screen: Megaboss Yellow 1/3/4/5, Hawkshroud Yellow 1, Ultramar Blue 1–4, Shading Medium, Celestine Skin 3–4, Carthalos Skin 5, Gnarlwood 1–4, Dataslate Grey 2–3, Necrolith Bone 2 and 4, Khemri Sands 2, plus your metallics.*

Metallics first: helmets, mail and polearm heads. Get the messy stuff out of the way before the tabards. I'm using Vallejo Air Steel for these. It just covers a bit better and doesn't splodge, so you don't get that sticky texture you sometimes get with metallics.

*Pencil line down the centre of a tabard, close-up.*

Then the tabards. I pencil a line straight down the middle, and the gold half goes on first: Megaboss Yellow 3 over that white zenithal. Then the blue half: Ultramar Blue 2. Yellow first, because if you get blue on yellow you're starting again, but yellow over blue just… vanishes. Ask me how I know.

*Brush close-up, folds.*

Then a thinned-down wash of Hawkshroud Yellow 1 over all the yellow, and this is the bit that made me go "ooh". It really warms everything up. The blue half gets Ultramar Blue 1 with Shading Medium in the folds. Shading Medium turns any Tone Pro paint into a wash, so you don't need a separate shade range.

Highlights: Megaboss Yellow 4 on the gold and Ultramar Blue 3 and 4 on the blue, raised folds only. And I'm keeping these scruffier than the knights on purpose. These lads have been sleeping in a turnip field. They're not meant to look like they've just come back from the dry cleaners.

Everyone's been talking about the coverage, and it really is great. One tip: thin it a touch out of the pot, especially on big flat areas, or you can end up with some thick patches. I was testing it neat to see what it's like, but normally I'd get the consistency right first.

At this point I was feeling pretty confident. Then I remembered this was the test model, and I had another nine to paint. Oh god.

## 8. Peasant bits, shields and the suffering — 8:10 to 9:40

*Speed up: hose, shirts, faces, hair, leather.*

Now the peasant bits. Hose and sleeves get mixed up across the unit, so they don't look like they all shop at the same Bretonnian Primark: Dataslate Grey, Gnarlwood 3 and Necrolith Bone 2. Undershirts are Necrolith Bone 2, shaded with Gnarlwood and Shading Medium, then Necrolith Bone 4 on top.

Skin is Celestine Skin 3, then Carthalos Skin thinned with Shading Medium as a shade, which is incredible. If anything, it's too potent, so next time I'll thin it down even more. Then Celestine Skin 4 on top. Hair and beards vary between Gnarlwood, Carthalos Skin and Khemri Sands. Belts are Gnarlwood too. They're peasants, so nobody's checking their cheekbones while a Knight of the Realm thunders past. Sorry, lads.

*Close-up: spear haft, then helmet.*

For the spears I did the same as the shield backs: dug out some reference images and went up the Gnarlwood tones. The fun bit is the wear. Where the hands grip the haft, I scrubbed the grain back so it looks worn smooth from years of peasants clinging on for dear life. Tiny detail, but it makes them look properly used.

The helmets get a rust effect: a stipple of Chotec Orange 1, then a very sparing stipple of Ta'unar Ochre 1 on top. Less is more here. You want "rusty old helmet", not "helmet made of Weetabix".

*No footage of the boots: use a still photo or skip.*

And the boots got a quick stipple of Blackstone Void 3 for a bit of grime. And that's the test model done. One down, nine to go.

*Shield close-up.*

And then the shields. I actually painted these first, because a nice flat surface is the perfect way to test a new paint. Same split as the tabards, gold half then blue half. For the fleur-de-lis, I started by marking it out in pencil. That was a mistake. So I switched to a fine brush and some Black Templar Contrast to outline it, then filled it in from there, gold on the blue side and blue on the gold. And honestly? I'm really pleased. The colours are so vibrant.

Then the back of the shield, which showed off the other great thing about Tone Pro: just going up the numbers. Gnarlwood 1, all the way up to 5, and you get this really cool wooden effect for barely any effort. A bit of black for some little knots in the wood, and now I'm weirdly excited to paint the other nine. I'm going to dig out some reference images and see how far I can push it. So far? I'd recommend Tone Pro to everybody. It's a great beginner paint, and it's great for more advanced painters too.

Now, I've still got nine more shields to go, so while I crack on with those, quick reminder that subscribing genuinely helps the channel massively. And if you want to support even further, there's a Buy Me a Coffee and affiliate links down below.

## 10. Airbrush verdict — 10:15 to 10:55

*Back at the desk, TurboX Mini in shot, "AD" on screen.*

So, the TurboX Mini after a full unit. Honestly, it did a really, really good job. It took the paint brilliantly, and the thing that surprised me most was how easy it was to clean. Anyone who's spent an evening picking dried primer out of a nozzle knows that's half the battle. The one thing I'd say: it would occasionally sputter, and I'd love a little more output from the compressor. But I'm nitpicking. So who's it for? Just about anyone after a workhorse airbrush. If you're a beginner, it's a great place to start. And if you're already established and you've got a compressor that puts out a bit more, hook this up to it and you'll avoid some of that speckling.
